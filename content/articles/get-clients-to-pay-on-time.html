Roughly half of freelance invoices are paid late. Most freelancers respond by waiting, then sending an apologetic email, then waiting again. That is treating a systems problem as an etiquette problem, and it is why the same clients are late every single time.

Why invoices are actually paid late

Very few clients are refusing to pay. In practice, late payment has four causes, and three of them are fixable before you ever send the invoice:

  • Your invoice is stuck. Wrong contact, missing purchase-order number, unapproved by a manager who never saw it. Enormously common in companies over about 50 people.
  • Your terms were never clear. If nobody agreed to net 30 in writing, you cannot be late — there was nothing to be late against.
  • You are in the slow queue. Accounts payable departments pay the people who chase and the people with consequences. Silence is interpreted as flexibility.
  • They genuinely cannot pay. The rarest case, and the only one that is really about money.

Fix it before the work starts

Take a deposit

Forty to fifty percent up front is standard professional practice in design, development, writing and consulting. It funds the work, and it filters out the clients who were never going to pay — a client who objects to a deposit is giving you information worth more than the project. The project quote calculator works out the deposit figure alongside the price.

Put four things in the contract

  1. Payment terms as a specific number of days from the invoice date, not “on completion”.
  2. A late fee clause: “Overdue amounts accrue interest at 1.5% per month.”
  3. A work-stops clause: “Work pauses on accounts more than 14 days overdue.”
  4. Who, by name and email, approves and pays invoices.

The fourth is the one nobody writes down and the one that prevents the most delays.

Ask the question that saves a month

At kickoff: “What does your payment process look like — do invoices need a PO number, and is there a payment run day?” Many companies pay only on the 1st and 15th. An invoice that arrives on the 16th is automatically two weeks late, and no amount of chasing changes that.

Design the invoice to be easy to pay

  • Send it the day the work is done. Delay on your side gives permission for delay on theirs.
  • Put the due date in bold, as a date. “Due 14 November 2026” beats “net 30”, which requires arithmetic and invites argument about when the clock started.
  • Include the PO number and the approver's name if you were given them.
  • Offer more than one payment method. Bank transfer and card. Every obstacle is a delay.
  • Number invoices sequentially and never reuse a number. Accounts payable systems reject duplicates silently.

The escalation sequence

Run it the same way every time, so it feels like a process rather than a confrontation.

3 days before due“Invoice 0042 is due Friday — anything you need from me?”Helpful
Day 1 overdueShort note with the invoice re-attachedNeutral, assume an oversight
Day 7Email the finance contact directly, copy your clientFactual

Day 45–60Final demand before collection or small claimsFormal, dated deadline

At day 30, calculate the interest properly rather than estimating it. “The balance is now $2,592.50, including $92.50 of contractual interest” moves an invoice up the queue in a way that “just following up” never does. Charge the fee, then decide whether to waive it

Apply it on the statement. Then, when they pay, you have a choice — and waiving it out loud is worth far more than never charging it: “I have waived the $92.50 interest this time.” You keep the relationship and you have established that the clause is real, which is exactly what makes the next invoice arrive on time.

While you wait, protect yourself

Late payment is a cash flow problem before it is an income problem. Two defences: keep a buffer of one to two months of expenses, and never let a single client exceed about 40% of your income. And keep your tax money separate from your operating cash — the quarterly tax set-aside calculator shows how much of each payment should move out of reach the day it lands, so a late invoice never becomes a missed tax payment.
