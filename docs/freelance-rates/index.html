Why the field changes the number

Two freelancers wanting the same income can need very different rates, and the reason is rarely talent. A photographer carries tens of thousands in equipment that loses value whether or not the phone rings. A copywriter carries almost no equipment but bills the fewest hours of anyone, because thinking does not fit on a timesheet. A voice actor spends more time auditioning unpaid than recording.

Those differences change the arithmetic before any judgement about value enters the room, which is why each page starts from its own costs and its own realistic working week rather than from one industry average that fits nobody.

Use it as a floor

None of these numbers is what you should quote. Each is the point below which accepting the work costs you money. What you add on top depends on the result you produce, how urgently it is wanted, and how few other people could deliver it — none of which a calculator can see.

Then stop selling hours

An hourly rate punishes you for getting faster: the better you become, the less a job earns. Use the figure to price the project, quote the project, and keep the hours to yourself.
