What the work costs you

The expenses box is prefilled with $1,800 a year, which for proofreaders typically goes on style manuals, proofreading software, professional training and accreditation. Replace it with your own total — every pound of genuine cost has to come out of revenue before any of it is yours.

Why 27 billable hours and not forty

Nobody pays you to write proposals, chase invoices, answer email or learn. The figure above assumes 27 genuinely billable hours a week, which is what a busy freelance proofreader actually logs once the unpaid work is excluded. Entering forty will produce a rate that looks competitive and loses money.

What moves the number up

In this field, rates rise with specialist subject areas, speed with consistency, and accreditation that clients recognise. None of those are about working faster, which is the point: an hourly rate rewards slowness, so use this figure to price the project rather than the hour. The quote calculator turns it into a fixed price with revisions and a deposit built in.
